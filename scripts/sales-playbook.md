**Target:** Indian-American (Patel) owned independent motels in the US (40-100 keys, budget/economy segment, often franchised under Choice / Wyndham / Best Western or fully indie roadside).
**Offer:** Compliance-safe soft-routing review system + reputation monitoring + monthly report.
**Pricing:** $497 setup + $499/month, 30-day cancel, performance guarantee.
**Operator:** Asia-based, native Hindi + English-fluent.
**Close channel:** WhatsApp same-day after the call.

> The numbers we anchor the pitch on are real and citable:
> - **Cornell School of Hotel Admin (Anderson):** A 1-point rise on a 5-point review scale lets a hotel raise ADR by **11.2%** at the same occupancy. A 1-point rise on the 100-point ReviewPro GRI = **+0.89% ADR, +0.54% occupancy, +1.42% RevPAR**.
> - **Harvard Business School (Luca, Yelp study):** A 1-star rise = **5–9% revenue lift** for independent properties (chains are unaffected — independents win the upside).
> - **AAHOA:** ~20,000 member-owned hotels, **~60%** of all US hotel properties, **70%** of Indian-American owners are surnamed Patel.
> - **Booking.com 2025 algorithm update:** Review score is now **recency-weighted** — reviews in the last 3 months drive your sort rank, reviews older than 24 months barely count. *This is the single best reason an owner needs fresh reviews every month, not just a high all-time average.*
> - **Post-stay SMS within 24 hours of checkout** = 2-3x the response rate of a 7-day email. SMS + email combined lands 20-30% review-request → review conversion.

## 1. THE Master Cold-Call Script

Three columns, side-by-side. The operator opens in English to clear the front desk, switches to Gujarati the second they hear a Patel voice. If the owner speaks Hindi-first (Marwari/UP families also own a chunk of motels), the operator slides to Hindi.

### 1a. The English Front-Desk Bridge (00:00 – 00:30)

> *Used to get past the night clerk / housekeeping / receptionist. Sounds like a vendor, not a cold caller.*

**Operator:**
> "Hi, good morning — this is **Rohan** calling for the owner of the property, **Mr. Patel**. I'm with **GuestStar Reviews**, we already work with a few motels in the area on their Google rating. Is **Bhai-saab** around, or should I try his cell?"

**Why it works:**
- "Mr. Patel" + "Bhai-saab" — front desk knows immediately you're "known", not a stranger. Front desks are trained to block strangers, not vendors who already know the family.
- "Work with a few motels in the area" — implies social proof without naming a competitor (which would get you hung up on).
- "His cell" — assumes you might already have it. Gatekeeper often just gives it: *"Yes, let me give you his number, hold on."*

**If gatekeeper says "He's not here":**
> "No problem — best time to catch him? I'll just call back. I don't want to leave a message because it's a quick 2-minute thing, not worth a callback for him."

> *Translation in head: "I'm not going to waste your boss's time, so just tell me when he's around."* Gatekeepers respect this. They will tell you "he comes in around 4 PM" or hand you the cell.

**If gatekeeper insists on a message:**
> "Sure — tell him **Rohan** from **GuestStar** called about his Google rating dropping below the neighbor's. He'll know what it means. I'll try back this evening."

> *The phrase "rating dropping below the neighbor's" creates a curiosity gap. The owner WILL call back, or at least pick up when you ring again.*
