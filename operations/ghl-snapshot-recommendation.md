## D. SaaS Mode vs Managed Service

For 6,000-10,000 independent Indian-American motel owners, **Managed Service is the correct model. Not SaaS Mode.**

Reasons:
1. **Tech anxiety.** First-generation Patel owners do not want to log in to a dashboard. They want an English/Hindi WhatsApp message at month-end saying "this month you got 14 new 5-star reviews." A SaaS Mode product asks them to log in, which fails the audience.
2. **Setup complexity.** SaaS Mode requires you to onboard each client into their own sub-account, hand them credentials, and provide ongoing support. Managed Service means YOU are the only login — you own the sub-account, the client never touches it. Lower support burden, fewer tickets, higher margin.
3. **Pricing power.** Managed Service at $499/mo + $497 setup grosses ~$6,500/yr per client. SaaS Mode at $97-197/mo grosses $1,200-2,400/yr per client. To match $499 MRR you'd need 2-5x the clients, all of whom expect software-grade self-service support. Brutal for a solo operator.
4. **The moat is YOU.** The competitive edge is a Hindi-speaking human who calls and texts the owner. SaaS Mode commoditises that to a login screen — undifferentiated from BirdEye, Podium, etc.

**Use SaaS Mode only when:** you cross 50+ managed-service clients and want a second-tier offering for owners who can't afford $499. Then spin up a $97-147/mo "self-serve" tier with the same snapshot, and let the client self-onboard. That's a Year 2 conversation.

If you do want SaaS-Mode-ready snapshots later: **Extendly's Ultimate Small Business Snapshot** and **HL Pro Tools' HL Snaps** are both designed for resale. Both bundle the SaaS onboarding videos that walk a client through their first login.

2. **Rewrite SMS/email copy for motel tone** (3 hrs). Replace generic "thanks for your service today" with stay-specific language: "Hope you had a comfortable stay at {motel_name}. If you have 30 seconds, your honest review on Google helps us a lot."
3. **Reskin to Beacon Reviews** (2 hrs). Custom values, sub-account logo, custom domain.
4. **Build monthly report template** (4 hrs). Use GHL's native Listings Performance Recap module + a custom PDF email template. WhatsApp-deliverable.
5. **Test end-to-end with a burner number** (2 hrs).
6. **Document the SOP for adding a new client** (2 hrs).

**Total: 16-18 hours of customisation work** — roughly one focused work-week for the operator.

## Compliance landmine (read this twice)

The review-gating workflow that ships with every reputation snapshot on the market — including LeadsFlex's, GHL Automations', GHL Experts', and even Extendly's — is built on the assumption that you route 4-5 star sentiment to Google and 1-3 star sentiment to a private form. **This is now illegal/non-compliant as of April 2026.**

Google's updated Business Profile review policy explicitly bans:
1. Asking for reviews on premises (kiosks/tablets at reception)
2. Staff review quotas
3. Asking for specific service mentions
4. Sentiment pre-screening (the "gating" you'll inherit from any snapshot)
5. Asking guests to mention specific staff names

FTC Consumer Review Rule enforcement began in early 2026 with civil penalties up to $53,088 per violation. Birdeye, Podium, Reputation.com, and GatherUp have all redesigned their products. GHL itself flags review-gating as a compliance risk in its own official documentation.

**For Beacon Reviews specifically:** the compliant flow is — every guest gets the same Google review request. There is no pre-screen. If a guest replies with a complaint, you offer the negative-feedback form as a service recovery channel AFTER the Google link has been sent. This still gives motel owners a private channel to address issues without violating policy.

Build this into the snapshot during your 16-hour customisation window. Do not deliver the snapshot as-is to a client.
